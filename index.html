SHEET 00 · START

Salt-loaded spray speeds up rusting (Designing Buildings), and it can reach well inland. This check records where your home is exposed, finds the sheltered spots rain never washes, grades every metal and concrete item you can see, including furniture inside and out, and keeps a washing and inspection schedule. Distance from the sea is context, not cause: you record what you see, you never diagnose.

Before you start - safety first

HSE
Railings and balconies: never lean on, climb or hang anything from a railing or balcony that shows rust at its fixings, loose parts, cracked or flaking concrete. Keep children off it and report it. (Professional practice)
Falling concrete: if pieces have fallen from a balcony, soffit or facade, keep people away from the area below and report it straight away. (Designing Buildings, Professional practice)
  • Look from the ground or a safe balcony. Stay off roofs and ladders: treat all roofs as fragile. (UK HSE Roof work)
  • Look only at corroded electrical fittings, meters, gas pipes and air-conditioner parts. Never touch, open or scrape them. (Professional practice)
  • Don't scrape, hammer or pick at rust or concrete to "see how bad it is". (Professional practice)

    What this tool will not do

    LIMITS
    • It does not measure metal loss, chloride levels in concrete or structural strength. Only engineers and specialists with the right tests can do that.
    • It does not say that salt caused a defect. Distance from the sea is recorded as context, not cause.
    • It does not replace a structural inspection, including any inspection required by law for apartment buildings in your area.
    • It never asks you to climb, scrape, open electrical or gas equipment, or test a railing by loading it.

    Standards & Measurements

    REFERENCES

    Guidance used in this tool

    • International corrosivity categories (ISO 9223): C4 includes a substantial chloride effect, for example less than 1 km (0.6 mi) from the ocean or within 100 m (330 ft) of sheltered coastal areas; C5 includes areas within a few hundred metres of the ocean and certain exposed areas along the coastline; CX includes the splash zone. (AGA ISO categories)
    • Why coasts: seawater and salt-loaded spray can accelerate rusting, and rust stays permeable, so the steel beneath keeps corroding. (Designing Buildings Rust)
    • Sheltered areas: parts of buildings exposed to rain may corrode more slowly than sheltered areas, because rain washes salt away. Owners of nearshore coastal buildings should inspect fasteners and metal connectors as regular maintenance. (FEMA TB 8)
    • Washing: hose down areas not regularly washed by rain at least every six months, more often in coastal areas, with mild soap and a thorough rinse (COLORBOND). Unwashed and high-risk areas of metal wall cladding every 3 months in the most severe environments, every 6 or 12 months in others (BRANZ). Wash stainless steel when a window would need washing (ASSDA). (COLORBOND, BRANZ, ASSDA)
    • Stainless steel: tea staining is cosmetic and does not affect structural integrity. It is most common within about 5 km (3.1 mi) of the surf; grade 316 or equivalent is the minimum there. (ASSDA)
    • Different metals: when two different metals touch and share moisture such as sea spray, one corrodes faster. Separate them with inert spacers or a durable paint system. (AGA Dissimilar metals)
    • Fixings: stainless steel and thick hot-dip galvanized (G185 or higher) connectors and fasteners improve corrosion protection in coastal areas. (FEMA TB 8)
    • Concrete: water, often with salt, entering concrete can cause spalling; act quickly, as the risk of damage rises the longer repair is delayed. (Designing Buildings Spalling)
    • Furniture and fittings (outdoor): powder-coated surfaces in coastal (very high) environments: clean every 3 months, compared with every 12 months in normal urban areas; mild detergent, soft brush, rinse with clean water; no aggressive solvents or citrus cleaners. Stainless steel: wash when a window would need washing. (Dulux Powder Coatings, ASSDA)
    • Furniture and fittings (indoor): dry environments are ideal for metals; corrosion accelerates above 65% RH. Damp causes rapid corrosion of metals above 75% RH, where salt deliquesces into a very corrosive solution. US EPA: keep indoor humidity below 60%, ideally 30-50%. (CCI Metal objects, CCI Humidity, EPA Mold & Moisture)
    • Air conditioners: clean evaporator and condenser coils; have a contractor do an annual check-up. (ENERGY STAR)
    • Units: 1 m = 3.28 ft; 1 km = 0.62 mi; 1 cm = 0.39 in. Metric first, imperial in brackets.
    Country-specific: New Zealand (BRANZ, NZS 3604): the high-risk zone is within 500 m (1,640 ft) of the sea or 100 m (330 ft) of tidal estuaries and sheltered inlets, and salt can travel more than 20 km (12 mi) inland. Southern Africa (HDGASA): corrosion of galvanized steel can be high generally within 1 km (0.6 mi) of the high-water mark. Australia (COLORBOND): product choice changes by distance from breaking surf, measured from the high-water mark. United States, Florida: condominium and cooperative buildings of three or more habitable storeys need a milestone structural inspection at 30 years (25 years where a local authority requires it, for example near salt water). Check your own national building rules.

    Items marked Professional practice reflect general inspection and HSE practice. No single public standard covers them, so no standard is claimed.
